Nothing's broken. Yet.

A shoelace comes undone on its own. The salt shaker is suddenly full of sugar. The remote turns up in the freezer. None of it is an accident — it's the work of devils no taller than a pencil, and they've made your house their playground.

You don't fight them. You just have to notice them in time, before whatever they're building toward finishes itself. Some are lazy and obvious. Some have been planning this for three rooms.

How it plays

Study the scene

Nothing points anything out for you. You scan a room the way you'd scan a room in real life — a little skeptical, a little slow.

Catch it before it finishes

Every devil is mid-plan. Tap it fast enough and its mischief undoes itself. Too slow, and that little disaster becomes permanent.

Rooms get busier

Later scenes hide two, then three, then a whole family of devils at once — and some of them are very good at looking like furniture.

Meet the household

Every devil in the game has a specialty. Here are three you'll learn to hate almost immediately.

The Untier

Specialty: shoelaces, ribbons, patience

Works slowly, one loop at a time, always right as you're about to leave the house.

The Swapper

Specialty: labels, remotes, left socks

Doesn't destroy anything — just moves it three feet away from where it should be, forever.

The Flicker

Specialty: lightbulbs, wifi, your patience

Small, twitchy, and convinced that every light in the house should flicker exactly once, at random.
